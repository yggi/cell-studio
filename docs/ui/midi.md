# MIDI

MIDI on this device: the connection, the ports, All notes off, MIDI out (every voice heard sent to a port), MIDI clock, MIDI learn (a controller's knob moving a `free()`) and MIDI in (keys playing a voice live) (#203, the epic; this page grows with it).
What a device is, which knob moves what and which cell the keys play belong to this browser, not to the code. Which output and channel a voice goes to belongs to this browser too, unless the piece says it with MIDI cards ([../language/midi.md](https://github.com/yggi/cells/blob/main/docs/language/midi.md), below).

- **Where.** MIDI is a page of the sidebar (the ☰ drawer, whose tabs are *Controls*, *MIDI*, *Forces* and *Files*; `src/ui/Sidebar.svelte`, `src/ui/MidiPanel.svelte`). The *MIDI* button in the transport and the picker's *Files ▾* button (the Files page, beside it) open it; a dot on its tab says whether a port is connected. The transport's button is also the **light** (`ui/MidiLight.svelte`, `ui/activity.ts`): a dot for the input and one for the outputs, each shown only while a port is chosen and dim while it is absent (the outputs' while every one chosen is); the input's flashes on any message from the device below system messages (so not the clock, active sensing or system-exclusive), the output's on each note or control sent, at the moment it sounds (notes go out ahead). It draws on the shared ticker only while a flash is pending or fading, so an idle connection costs no frames. Its groups, top to bottom: the connection (Connect), *Outputs* (each a device, *No MIDI output* or a port, with its clock and its drum map, and ×; a button for each port plugged in that no output sends to yet, which adds it), *Input* (*No MIDI input*, *All inputs* or a port; Refresh ports; All notes off), *This piece* (the routing matrix, the kit's notes, the built-in sound), *Pitch* (each output's bend range and MPE), *Keys*, *Learn*. Under them, in words, what was found or why there is no connection: no Web MIDI in this browser (Safari, Firefox without its add-on), or access blocked (a denied prompt, an iframe). Without MIDI everything else works as before.
- **Kept.** The ports chosen are kept in this browser by id and by name (`storageKey('cell-studio:midi')`): the outputs, in order, for every piece; the routing, per piece. A store from before the matrix is read as one: its output as the first, each piece's channels and kit channel on it, a device a piece's slot n was sent to as the nth output. A port chosen before (any output, or the input) is connected again on load without a click, but only when the browser says MIDI is already granted (`navigator.permissions`; a browser that can't say is taken as granted): otherwise the page says so and waits for Connect, so a dismissed prompt doesn't come up again on every visit. A first visit asks for nothing until Connect. A port is found by its id, else by its name; with two ports of one name, the first listed is taken.
- **Unplugged and plugged in again.** Every change of the ports adopts the chosen ones again, since a port plugged in again is a new object: the output by its id, or, when that id is gone, a port with the same name (ids change on replug on some systems), and the input listened to anew. A chosen port that is absent shows as *Not connected*.
- **All notes off** sends CC 123 to every output, on every channel sent on there since the last time (on all 16 when none was), and forgets them. It runs by itself when the tab comes back into view, since a tab frozen while hidden can leave a release unscheduled and hardware sounding, and its button is live whenever any output is connected.

## MIDI out

With an output chosen, every note the voices play goes out too, to each output and on each channel the routing says: the player hands each window's notes, on the audio clock, to its `NoteSink` beside the voices (`audio/player.ts`), so what is sent is what sounds, mutes and `off` included. A send (a voice played only into a resonator or a tape) is not heard from itself and is not sent.

- **When.** Each message is timestamped for the moment the built-in sound plays that note *heard*, not made (decision 005): `midi/clock.ts` reads the audio clock against `performance.now()` from the output timestamp's pairing (once the output has run 0.2 s; it follows a dropped buffer within three readings, and a leap of more than 50 ms, another device or a context resumed, at once), and the pictures' lag (`audio/heard.ts`, through `audio/device.ts` `heardLatency`) reads the same estimate. A timestamp is never in the past.
- **The routing matrix.** *This piece* is a matrix: a row for each gate of the piece ([mix.md](https://github.com/yggi/cells/blob/main/docs/language/mix.md): a gate) and each voice it plays directly, in the order it declares them, a row for its kit, and a column for each output. At each crossing, the channel the row goes out on there, or *—*: not sent there. So any voice can go to any device, on any channel, to several at once. A gate is one part, all its receivers on its channel (`&leadOut play=&tune [&lead, &bell]` sends both on one: layering is the synth's), so naming a gate is how a line is routed to gear by name. By default everything is on the first output: the gates and voices numbered 1, 2, 3 … skipping the kit's channel (10, General MIDI's, or the one chosen for it there), wrapping round to 1 after the fifteenth (a sixteenth voice shares channel 1); on any other output nothing goes until a crossing is set. Crossings are kept per piece (by its title) in this browser (`routes`, by output); removing an output forgets what was routed to it. A kit read beside a pitch term (orchestration, a transposed groove: [../language/midi.md](https://github.com/yggi/cells/blob/main/docs/language/midi.md)) is no drum part: each member it picks is a row of its own (its gate's, through one) and goes out at its pitch. Plain drums (a kit read with `over=`, a groove's voices, a gate's kit) go wherever the kit row says, on each output as its drum map makes them; a rotation leaves the kit's channel out. MIDI in's on-screen pads, the computer's drum keys and a controller's kit channel follow the kit's channel on the first output (`Keys.drumChannel`; 10 when it goes on none there); thru and a learned knob's echo go to the first output.
- **Outputs.** Added from the buttons under them (one for each port plugged in that no output sends to yet: *+ Juno-X*), up to 16, and removed with × (never the last; the first removed, the next is first). Each sends through a `MidiOut` and a `ClockOut` of its own; another device chosen, or the output removed, silences it first (All notes off on what it sent, and Stop to its clock).
- **A piece with MIDI cards** (`map=midi`, [../language/midi.md](https://github.com/yggi/cells/blob/main/docs/language/midi.md)) sets its routing itself: each note goes out on every channel its cards' entries put it on, and nothing else is sent (the default routing above doesn't send the voices the cards leave out). A card's output slot (`map=midi(10, 2)`; 1 when it says none) is the output of that number in the list: the matrix shows each crossing the cards set, read only, with a column for every slot the piece uses (one past the outputs has *No device: not sent* until a device is chosen, which adds it). Rotation (MPE, below) is off for such a piece. A voice only on a MIDI card is sent and not heard: the player hands its notes to MIDI out marked `unheard`, and the built-in voices leave them out, whether the built-in sound is on or off.
- **Drums.** A kit member's note is General MIDI, picked by its name: `bd`/`kd`/`kick` 36, `rim` 37, `sd`/`snare` 38, `cp`/`clap` 39, `hh`/`ch` 42, `ph` 44, `oh` 46, `crash` 49, `ride` 51, `lt`/`lo` 41, `mt`/`mid`/`tom` 45, `ht`/`hi` 48 (a trailing number is ignored); any other name takes the next free note from 36. A device's own map is applied only at the output (`midi/drum-presets.ts`), each output its own (*Drums:* under it, when the piece has a kit): *GM* sends them as they are, *Novation Circuit* onto its four drum notes 60, 62, 64 and 65, and a note typed in the matrix (a member's row, under each output the kit goes to) makes it *Custom*. The map is kept per device, by the port's name.
- **Velocity** is the note's level at its start times the louder of its gains, as a square root (synths mostly turn velocity into amplitude on a square law), onto 1–127.
- **Pitch.** A note's frequency is the nearest key against A4 = 440 and the cents it is off it; a 12-EDO piece sends no bend at all. A microtonal note (`map=edo|ed(r)|ratios`) sends a bend before its note-on, and a note's glides go out as bends (a ramp every 20 ms), sent only up to the scheduling horizon. A `glide=` voice's run goes out as its notes, legato: each held into the next by the slide (at most the next one's length; into a repeat of its key only up to it), the overlap a synth with legato or portamento glides on; the run's later notes are struck as the horizon reaches them, so Stop leaves none behind (`legatoNotes`, `engine/midi.ts`; #334). The bend range is a setting of each output, in *Pitch* (±2 by default, ±48 for MPE synths), kept per device by the port's name (`bends`; a device with none of its own takes the one kept before outputs had their own). A bend is per channel, so notes with different bends that overlap on one channel sound out of tune: the page says so, naming the output, and *MPE* on an output rotates the notes sent there over a range of its channels (2–16 by default, the kit's channel there left out), each note on the channel free longest; kept per device the same way (`rotates`). An MPE synth and a mono synth on two outputs each get their own.
- **Note-offs.** Each channel and key remembers who holds it (start and end). A note-off goes only when nobody still holds that moment; a key struck again while held retriggers (off, then on).
- **Stop, a seek, All notes off.** A timestamped message can't be taken back (`MIDIOutput.clear()` isn't in Chrome), and notes go out up to the look-ahead (0.3 s, 0.12 s for pieces with hands) plus a window (0.25 s at the code's tempo) before they sound. So silencing sends All notes off at once on every channel in use, a note-off for every note still held (at once for one sounding, at its start for one already sent ahead, which All notes off now can't reach), and a bend back to the centre where one was left, after any bend sent ahead. Nothing later goes, so Play again, or a seek (a pause, then a start elsewhere), within the look-ahead isn't cut: where a note sent since sounds across a note-off left ahead, it is struck again just after it (a retrigger, not a cut).
- **Edits while playing.** A recompile (an edit, a mute, a held `free()` moved) swaps the plan at the next window and the routing with it; notes already held stay held and get their note-offs, as on the voices. Only a routing that says something else (a voice put on another channel or output, a voice added, an output removed) silences first, as Stop does; so does a change of bend range or rotation, and choosing another device for an output silences the old one.
- **Built-in sound too.** Off, the built-in sound is muted at its output while MIDI plays on: the audio clock stays the master and the pictures still move. Hands (strike, bow) are not sent to MIDI, so with it off they are silent. The same goes for notes sounded by turning a piece's timeline by hand (`Player.scroll`): they go to the built-in voices, never to the MIDI output.
- **Unplugged while playing.** A send to a port that vanished is dropped (every send sits in a try); when it is plugged in again it is adopted, and the next notes go to it.

## MIDI clock

*Clock* under an output (off by default, kept per device by the port's name) sends it Timing Clock (0xF8) at 24 a beat, so a drum machine or a sequencer can follow the piece (#206).

- **Which beat.** The piece's main meter: the clock of its first played cell, followed up its `step=` chain to the meter with a rate of its own (sailor's `&bar`). A beat is one of its steps, or as many as `beat=` or `meter("4/4", 2)` say: `scan(4 4 4 4)` is four beats a bar, 96 ticks. A piece whose first cell steps at a bare rate with nothing stepping with it (`&tune step=4 0 2 4`, the telephone, the 5 to 12) has no meter and sends no clock; the page says so.
- **Laid through the meter's time map.** Tick k falls at the meter's step k·beat/24 through its time map (`engine/clocking.ts`), worked out from its index, never by adding up intervals (`engine/midiclock.ts`). So the ticks swing with `swing=`, bend with a scan (`scan(27 23 26 24)`), and speed up and slow down with a motor: spinning up, wow, running down; none go after a clock has come to rest or a piece has ended (`laps=`). A belted cell is not the meter; the meter it follows is.
- **When.** Each window's ticks go with its notes, in the same callback and through the same clock offset (`midi/clock.ts`), so they are timestamped for the heard moment as the notes are; never in the past, and never earlier than the clock message before them.
- **Start, Continue, Stop.** Start (0xFA) goes 2 ms before the first tick when play begins at the piece's start (from Stop, or paused at 0). Anywhere else (Play after Pause, a seek, the clock switched on while playing) the run begins on the next 16th, and a Song Position Pointer (0xF2, in 16ths from the piece's start) then Continue (0xFB) go before its first tick, so a device following picks up where the piece is. Stop (0xFC) goes on Pause, Stop, a seek and at a piece's end, and when the clock is switched off or another device is chosen for the output. Ticks are sent ahead (the look-ahead plus a window) and can't be taken back, so Stop lands just after the last tick already sent, at most about half a second after now: no tick follows it. The next run waits until after that Stop, so Play again soon after Pause leaves the device stopped for that long while the piece plays on, then picks up in step.
- **A tempo moved while playing.** A held `free()` tempo, a hand on a motor or an edit lays the next window's ticks on the new grid. Where two grids meet, a tick nearer the last one than half its own spacing is left out, so none is doubled, and none is added. Moving the tempo also moves where the piece stands in beats (time is kept in seconds, so the beat at a moment changes, and the notes jump with it); a device following keeps counting from its own downbeat, so Pause and Play lines them up again.
- **A lossy grid.** 24 ticks a beat carry a 16th (6 ticks) and a triplet 8th (8), but not a quintuplet 16th (19.2) nor anything a bent scan puts between ticks (Jambot `docs/language-time.md` T3). The clock only counts time; the notes still go out where they are written.

## MIDI learn

A controller's knob, fader or pad moves a `free()` value exactly as its slider does ([../language/free.md](https://github.com/yggi/cells/blob/main/docs/language/free.md)); nothing is written into the piece.

- **Learn.** With MIDI connected and an input chosen, every `free()` control (the drawer and a cell's sheet; not a text) has *Learn*. Press it, then move a knob or press a pad: the next control change or note-on binds to it (Esc, or *Learn* again, cancels). A bound control shows its knob (*CC 7*, *note 36*, *CC 7 ch 2* off the first channel), and *Forget* (×) lets go. A knob learned for a second control leaves the first. The MIDI page says how many controls of the open piece are learned, with *Forget all*. A wait for a knob ends unbound when its control goes (its sheet closed), another piece is opened (or this one renamed), or the input goes; Esc is then everyone's again.
- **Moving.** CC 0–127 runs from `lo` to `hi` through the control's own scale (`src/ui/free.ts`, `freeAt`/`freePosition`, which the slider uses too): straight in the slider's steps (whole bpm for tempo), logarithmic for a frequency or a rate (a setting in Hz such as `lpf=`, `hpf=`, `tone=`, `wanderhz=`, which `engine/attributes.ts` (in `@yggi/cells`) marks; a clock's `step=` or `loop=` not in bpm) over a positive range of three octaves or more (`200..5000`: the middle is 1000), both ends exact; any other setting (`drive=`, `index=`) runs straight. The value then goes to `piece.hold`, as the slider's does, so it plays until kept and Stop lets go of it.
- **Choices.** A labelled `free()` (a keypad, a `meter()` choice) takes a knob in equal parts, one a choice; or pads: the pad learned picks the first choice, the pads above it on its channel the next ones (sixteen pads for a telephone keypad). On a slider, pads step from `lo` the same way.
- **Nothing jumps.** Soft takeover: a knob that isn't where the value is leaves it until it passes through it (or lands within a knob's step of it); until then the slider shows the gap, a band from the value to a mark where the knob is. Once something else moves the value (the slider, Stop, Keep, opening the piece again), the knob takes over afresh.
- **Cost.** Each hold compiles the piece again, so a fast knob holds at most once an animation frame per control: the last value before the frame. A tab that draws no frames (hidden, or behind another window) while MIDI keeps arriving holds within 50 ms instead.
- **Kept.** Bindings are this browser's (a controller is a device), kept by piece title and by the `free()`'s place as its cell and what it sets (`bar/step`, `fx/param:lpf`; a second of a kind in one cell `mix/gain#1`), not by its place in reading order, which an edit above it moves (`storageKey('cell-studio:midi-learn')`). A piece's title edited in place carries its bindings to the new title once the title has rested (about 0.8 s, so a title half typed leaves nothing behind); a title that already has bindings of its own keeps them and takes none, and a piece saved or opened under another title starts with none. Nothing is pruned: an entry for a piece nobody opens again stays (a few bytes). Another browser has none. A renamed cell loses its bindings, and so does an unnamed one, `&( … )`, which is named by its line and column: a line inserted above it, or text before it on its line, moves it. They bind, they hold nothing, so Stop clearing what was held leaves them.
- **Echo out.** *Send a slider's moves back to its knob* in the MIDI page (off by default) sends a learned CC back to its controller when its slider or keypad is moved by hand, for motor faders or LED rings: to the output port with the name of the input the knob was learned from (most controllers are an input and an output of one name), whether or not it is one of the page's outputs; never to a synth. A controller with no output port of its name gets nothing, and the page says so under the setting. A knob learned before controllers were remembered (it has no `from`) echoes to the first output, as it did. A controller that sends it back moves the value to the knob's nearest of its 128 steps, so with echo on the slider is quantised to 128 steps (some bpm of a wide range can't be reached).

## MIDI in

A keyboard plays the piece's voices live, stopped or playing (#207). Nothing is written into the code; recording a braid left free is [below](#recording-a-braid-left-free).

- **Hands, not the plan.** A key is a hand on a voice, beside strike, damp and bow: `press(voice, freq, velocity)` gives an id and `release(id)` lets it go (`audio/player.ts`, `audio/voices.ts`, `audio/live.ts`). The note is held with the voice's own envelope until released, started at once (the context's `currentTime`, a few milliseconds on, not through the look-ahead; a key let go before that still sounds, then goes). A press takes a stopped transport to paused, as every hand does ([transport.md](transport.md)); a release with no press is ignored.
- **Pause and Stop.** Pause lets go of the plan's notes and leaves a key held sounding (it is a hand's, and the hand is still down); it goes into its release when the key comes up. Stop cuts it with everything else, and the keys forget what was down, so a key let go after Stop does nothing.
- **Which cell: the armed one.** *Play with* in the MIDI page chooses a cell, or leaves it to the cell whose sheet is open; with neither, the first cell that plays, in reading order (a melody, else a voice, before a kit). Arming is this page's state, not the code's. The voice armed and its melody light live as they sound (`ui/context.ts` `nodeState`), and the voice's wheel shows its sound.
- **The key through the tuning.** A melody (or a voice, through the first melody that plays it) sounds a key at the nearest pitch the melody can play: its numbers read through its table (`over=`), else its grid, down to the grid's tuning (`map=edo`, `ed(r)`, `ratios`), against the voice's own pitch and A4 (`engine/keys.ts`). Over a pentatonic, F sounds E and F♯ sounds G; over 19-EDO, a key takes the nearest of the 19 the table has; a key midway takes the lower. *A key outside the scale, played and recorded* is one setting for live play and takes alike (#210, [decision 018](https://github.com/yggi/cells/blob/main/docs/decisions/018-free-braid.md)): *Snap* (the default) as just said; *Allow* sounds every key at its own equal-tempered pitch (a take writes it with an accidental); *Ignore* sounds a key on the table at its pitch and one off it not at all. #207's *plays as played*, kept in this browser, reads as *Allow*, its *nearest* as *Snap*. The on-screen keys follow it too, since they go through the same `press`. A voice no melody plays sounds as played.
- **Kits.** A controller plays the kit on every channel the kit goes out on, on any output (`Midi.drumChannels`; the kit on 10 on the first and on 3 on the second: 10 and 3), or with a kit armed; the on-screen pads and the computer's drum keys are on its channel on the first output (10 by default; on a piece with MIDI cards, the channel its card gives it there; 10 when it goes on none). They play the kit's members by General MIDI drum note, the same notes MIDI out sends (`bd` 36, `sd` 38, `hh` 42 …). A device's note comes back through **its own** drum map first, the one kept under the name it sends as (`Midi.drumsFrom`, `midi/drum-presets.ts` `gmNotesFor`; General MIDI if it has none): a Circuit played in under the *Novation Circuit* preset has its pad 62 play the first member that is a rim, snare, clap or tom, whichever output it is.
- **The mixes' wheels hear a key** (#252): a key held on a voice opens the taps of every mix wheel that voice goes through (`Gate.hold` in `audio/gate.ts`, from `press`), from the press until the key has rung out after its release (or Stop), as a note of the plan does for its own mixes; a plan note playing meanwhile can't close a tap under it. A resonator struck or bowed needs none: its taps are always open.
- **Velocity** gives the level, on the square law MIDI out uses the other way (`keyLevel`): a key played in at velocity 90 and sent out again is sent at 90. **The sustain pedal** (CC 64, any channel) holds releases until it comes up; a key it holds struck again strikes again. **All notes off** and **All sound off** from the controller (CC 123 and CC 120, any channel) let every key go and the pedal up, so a lost note-off can't leave a note sounding; Stop does the same, and so do *All notes off* in the page, the page coming back into view (a key let go while it was hidden sent its note-off to no one), choosing another input, and the input listened to being unplugged (`Midi.letGo`).
- **Two inputs** under *All inputs* are two sets of keys: a key is held under its input's name as well as its channel and note, so one device letting go of a note leaves the other's sounding, and one of them unplugged lets go of its keys only (found by name: two devices of one name are not told apart). A kit put on channel 1 makes a controller's channel 1 the pads (as 10 does by default); the computer's and the screen's keys are on no channel, so they stay melodic. **Thru** (a setting, with an output chosen): every key echoed at once, without a timestamp, wherever the routing matrix sends the voice it plays (`Midi.thruOf`, `Midi.sendTo`, so All notes off reaches it): each output and channel on its row (its gate's, for a voice played through one), as the note it sounds (a key snapped to the scale goes out snapped; a microtonal one as the nearest key, unbent), a kit's member as its drum note through each output's drum map. A key that sounds nothing, or plays a voice that goes out nowhere, sends nothing. The pedal goes to every output and channel the armed voice's row has. Each key's note-off goes where its note-on went, whatever the routing has become meanwhile; switching thru off, Stop and every let-go above first send a note-off for each key down (and the pedal up), so no output is left holding them. Thru sounds as the piece will when it plays; to play a device the piece doesn't use, route the voice to it.
- **MIDI learn and the keys.** Both hear the input through `Midi.hear`, learn first. A message learn claims is not played (`Learn.claims`): any control change or note-on while a control waits for a knob, a CC learned, a pad learned or one above it within its control's choices. So CC 64 is the pedal unless it is learned for a slider (then it moves the slider only); forget it and it is the pedal again.
- **The computer keys** (*Play with the computer keys*, off by default, so the page keeps its keys): A to L the white keys and W E T Y U O the black ones from C4 to D5, 1 to 7 the kit (kick, snare, closed hat, open hat, pedal hat, rim, ride). A key typed into a field or the code editor (a contenteditable), a repeat or a chord with a modifier is left alone; a key down when the window loses focus lets go. The letters are read as typed (`KeyboardEvent.key`), so on a layout other than QWERTY they move with it (AZERTY's A is Q's place) and a Cyrillic layout plays none.
- **On-screen keys** (the dock's keyboard and pads, [keys.md](keys.md); the piano roll's key column, #211) go through the same path. A row of a cell: `studio.keys.pressRow(cell, value, velocity?)` and `studio.keys.releaseRow(cell, value)`, where `value` is a number written in the cell (an entry with laps), sounding as the cell plays it whatever is armed (`engine/keys.ts` `rowNote`: through its tables to its grid's tuning, on its out's voice; a kit's row is its member); `studio.keys.heldRows(cell)` (reactive) is the rows down, the pedal's included. A plain keyboard: `studio.keys.press(note, velocity?, channel?)`, `release(note, channel?)` and `held` (the notes down, any channel but the kit's). Both take the pedal, strike again when pressed again, and go out through thru (a row as the MIDI note nearest).
- **Latency.** In headless Chromium a live note reaches the voice's analyser about 7 ms after the message (the smoke test's median of five), and the output adds its own (42 ms there, `baseLatency + outputLatency`). On a phone it is #154's measurement; `balanced` against `interactive` (#91) matters most here.

## Recording a braid left free

A take records the keys into a braid left free, `free(0 2 4 ~ …)` ([../language/free.md](https://github.com/yggi/cells/blob/main/docs/language/free.md#a-braid-left-free), [decision 018](https://github.com/yggi/cells/blob/main/docs/decisions/018-free-braid.md), #210). It is held, not written, until Keep.

- **What it records into.** The armed cell, when its braid is left free and it plays notes on a voice (not a kit); with a voice armed, the first such cell that plays it. **●** (record) is on the transport for a piece with a braid left free, greyed out while nothing is armed that it can record into.
- **One pass.** ● arms a take and starts play. The next whole pass of the cell's loop is taken (a pass just begun, within half a step, counts): ● blinks while it waits and is lit while it takes. ● again, Pause or Stop calls it off and holds nothing.
- **When a key went down.** Each key carries its event's `timeStamp` (a MIDI message's, a key event's; the on-screen keys take the moment they are pressed). It goes through the clock offset (`midi/clock.ts`, the one MIDI out's timestamps use) to the moment heard on the audio clock, and through the transport to where the piece was heard then (`Player.heardAt`). That is mapped to the cell's step through its time map (`engine/clocking.ts`), so a swung, bent or motor-driven clock is undone. Positions stay exact until the pass is over; then each key starts on its nearest step (half a step rounds later), and a key starting outside the pass is dropped.
- **What is written** (`engine/capture.ts`). A key is the number the cell writes for its pitch, through its table and tuning, under the setting above: *Snap* the nearest entry, *Allow* the nearest step of the grid below as an entry with accidentals (`3#`: the fewest, sharps before flats), *Ignore* nothing for a key off the table. A row of the cell's piano roll is its own number. Keys starting on one step are a chord `[0,4]` as long as the longest, a key held over steps `4@2` (no further than the next key), a step nothing covers `~`. The sustain pedal sounds on, but a note ends at its key's release.
- **Held.** At the pass's end the take is held in the braid's place (one recompile a pass), shown in the cell's sheet with **Keep** and **Revert**, and the transport's *Save* and *Revert* take it with the rest of what is held. Another take replaces it; Stop lets go of it.

## MIDI files

A piece's notes saved as a Standard MIDI File (#307, the first step of opening one and shortening it): **Save its notes
as MIDI** in the Files page.

- **What is written** is what MIDI out sends (decision 005): the same channel table, routes, keys, velocities and bends
  (`engine/midi.ts`), a track a channel named after its voices, a kit on channel 10 as its drum notes. A kit's track is named after its
  members in order, each with its pitch where it has one of its own (`bd(A1) dundun(C2) shekere`; a key, or Hz between
  keys), so the file says which drum each note is and how it is tuned (#334). A note no MIDI
  key can carry is left out, and the status line says how many.
- **Time** is the main meter's (`engine/midiclock.ts`): a quarter note a beat (`beat=` steps), each step a fixed number
  of ticks and the tempo its length, so a scan's or a motor's tempo map is the file's tempos and a note on a step is on
  the grid (BWV 847: 1,765 notes, every one on a sixteenth, at its time within 2 ms). A swing is tempos that alternate.
  With no main meter, 120 bpm. A piece that ends is written whole, a loop one cycle, an hour at most.
- **Reading** (`engine/midifile.ts` `readMidi`): format 0 and 1, notes (a note-on at velocity 0 ends one; a key struck
  again while held ends the earlier strike), bends, programs, tempos, time signatures, track names, the copyright
  notice; SMPTE timing as seconds. Text (names, the notice) is written as UTF-8 and read as UTF-8 where it is that,
  else as Latin-1. The notice is a line of the text of its own (`copyright "…"`), saved back into the file's copyright
  field; a file converted to cells says it as a comment under the card.
- **Opening one** ([decision 025](../decisions/025-midi-documents.md); *Open a file…* takes `.mid` too) shows it as it
  is: its text in the code editor (`engine/miditext.ts`): a header, the tempos and meters, then a track at a time, a
  line an event at bar:beat:tick (`1:1:0 ch1 C5 120 v90`; keys by name, sharps or flats; `program`, `bend` lines). It
  plays as it is (its notes written out behind the scenes, a fixed tempo); edit it there (a line it can't read is an
  error on that line), save it (*Save the MIDI file*: a `.mid` again, event for event). Its graph is its **machine**
  ([decision 034](../decisions/034-midi-machine.md)): the cells it plays through, read only (*Convert to cells first*).
  **Convert to cells** (the button over the machine, the strip under the editor, the Files page) folds it, then replays
  the fold as it comes about, a cell at a time ([notes/conversion.md](https://github.com/yggi/cells/blob/main/docs/notes/conversion.md)), and writes it as
  a piece (`engine/midiimport.ts` `cellsFromMidi`), every note kept, folded by `engine/fold` where that plays the same
  and is shorter ([the folder](https://github.com/yggi/cells/blob/main/docs/notes/fold.md): figures over chord tables, phrases and their transpositions as cells,
  slower clocks):
  - *The grid*: the coarsest step every start falls on (a quarter note, an eighth, a triplet … a 64th; within a 96th
    of a quarter and an eighth of the step); a file played in, on none, is laid on sixteenths, and the status line says
    how many notes moved.
  - *The time*: one meter, `&tempo scan(…) step=free(…bpm) beat=… laps=1`, a step a grid step, each as long as the
    file's tempo map makes it (a scan's steps are its weights against their mean; the commonest length weighs 1), so
    the piece ends where the file does and its tempo is a slider.
  - *The lines*: a line a track and channel, named after the track; notes that start and end together are a chord, one
    that overlaps goes in a line beside (`&piano2`); a melody in semitones from C4 on a triangle voice at C4; channel 10
    as drum voices by their General MIDI names (`&bd`, `&sd`, `&hh` …; a drum with no voice is left out and counted).
    A drum track the app saved (named as above, every note one of its members' notes) comes back as the piece's own
    drums: each note its member, a voice at the member's pitch (a General MIDI name's sound, else a sine where it has
    a pitch and a noise where it hasn't). Why the file and not the export: a kit's drums stay drum notes on channel
    10 for a drum machine; their tuning is only the app's to read back, so it rides in the track's name, which every
    program keeps and none plays (#334).
    Each line runs to the end with a rest, so nothing comes round again.
  - *A legato line* (#334): a line of single notes, each held into the next by one slide in seconds (or by the next
    one's length where that is shorter; one on the same key only meets it), is a glide's run as MIDI out sends it: its
    notes are tied again (each ending where the next starts) and its voice gets `glide=` that slide. Overlaps that differ
    (a file played in) are notes that overlap, as before.
  - *Not kept*: velocities, bends, programs, controllers.
  - BWV 847 saved as MIDI and opened again: all 1,765 notes, starts and lengths within 10 ms, in 5 lines, 6,805
    characters (the example: 9,810 with its labels and notes). Today's Shorten folds that to 5,012 in 6.6 s.
  - *Watched*: the folder runs in a worker (`ui/fold.worker.ts`) and is watched as it runs (`cellsFromMidi`'s `watch`,
    `engine/fold` `Watch`: each pair made, each phrase and its copies found, each symbol taken back, the piece as it is
    by then). The graph shows each step, framed as it grows, replayed at a pace to follow (one at least every 110 ms,
    skipping ahead where they pile up: a fold of under a second is seen in a few); the strip at its foot says the round
    and the move, **Stop** keeps the piece as far as it got. At the end, the fold taken: the one with variants or the
    one without (`STRUCTURE`), or the notes written out if a fold doesn't play the same.
  - *In degrees* ([decision 033](https://github.com/yggi/cells/blob/main/docs/decisions/033-degrees.md), #344): the folder folds the piece twice, in semitones
    and in degrees of its key, and keeps the shorter. The key is the scale (major, minor or harmonic minor, on any
    tonic) its notes need fewest accidentals over, a tie going to the one whose tonic the bass ends on
    (`engine/fold/degrees.ts`). Folded in degrees, the piece declares it (`&key scale(minor, C)`), each line reads
    through it (`over=&key`), a note is its degree (`6#`: the seventh raised a semitone) and a phrase comes back a step
    of the scale higher as itself moved (`&m3^1`), as a sequence does. Figures over chord tables are looked for in
    semitones only. The status line says which: *folded … in degrees of C minor*.
  - *In sections* (#346): where the piece's key changes and stays, each line is cut, each part a cell read through its
    section's key (`&upB over=&key2`), the line splicing its parts in order (`&up step=&tempo &upA &upB@2 &upC`). It
    is taken where at most 15 % longer than the shortest fold without (it reads as the piece's form); the status line
    names the sections' keys.
  - *In voices* (#366): where a track holds several voices (a fugue's hands), the notes are given to voices
    (`fold/voices.ts`: as many as sound at once, each note to the free voice it moves least) and folded a line a voice
    (`&soprano`, `&alto` …), taken where shorter than a line a track: a subject stays in one line, whichever hand
    plays it.
  - *At another speed* (#345): a passage of 12 steps or more whose notes all share a length (eighths among sixteenths)
    is a cell at its own speed, spliced slower: `&m3^$-7@2`; the same passage at two speeds is one cell.

## The MIDI library

MIDI files that come with the app, in collections: each a cell of its own in the library, stacked to the left of the
wheel (the tutorial is to its right), and a section of the cards. For now four of Bach's, as the
[Mutopia Project](https://www.mutopiaproject.org/) has them, each file as published (or, where it has repeats, built from
its LilyPond source with the repeats played: `tools/mutopia.py`) with its typesetter and its licence (public domain or
CC BY-SA) in its collection's README:

- **Well-Tempered Clavier** ([midi/wtc/](https://github.com/yggi/cells/blob/main/midi/wtc/README.md)): 37 of the 96 preludes and fugues;
- **Inventions and Sinfonias** ([midi/inventions/](https://github.com/yggi/cells/blob/main/midi/inventions/README.md)): all 30;
- **Anna Magdalena Notebook** ([midi/anna/](https://github.com/yggi/cells/blob/main/midi/anna/README.md)): its 19 keyboard pieces, the minuets and
  polonaises among them, their repeats played;
- **Musical Offering** ([midi/offering/](https://github.com/yggi/cells/blob/main/midi/offering/README.md)): the Ricercar a 3 and the Ricercar a 6.

Each collection's `voices/` has its files again with a track a voice, the ground truth of the disassembler's bench
([notes/disassembler.md](https://github.com/yggi/cells/blob/main/docs/notes/disassembler.md)); the app doesn't ship them.

`src/corpus.ts` lists them (`MIDI_SETS`, each `midi/<set>/index.json`); a collection's name is a tag of its files and
the entry's `collection`, which puts it in its cell (`layout/library.ts` `sideOf`). A file is fetched when it opens, and
opens as any MIDI file does, as text, playing, for **Convert to cells**. Its card's wheel is drawn from its notes as
cells, written out.
Each file's credit (`src/corpus.ts` `creditOf`: the piece, who typeset it, its licence linked, its Mutopia page) is in
its details in the library, in its copyright field (the text's `copyright "…"` line, so a `.mid` saved from it carries
it), and as comments at the top of the cells it converts to; a CC BY-SA file's say that what is made from it shares
that licence.
Every one also carries the tag `MIDI file` (`engine/tags.ts` `MIDI_FILE`). The folder over them: the probe
`tools/probe/midi.test.ts`, the ratchet `engine/fold/midiRatchet.ts` (in `@yggi/cells`) ([notes/fold.md](https://github.com/yggi/cells/blob/main/docs/notes/fold.md)).

## The code

`src/midi/` is the browser glue (`access.ts` the Web MIDI connection, `routing.ts` adopting the ports, `surfaces.ts` reading a message, `out.ts` sending the notes played, `clock-out.ts` sending the clock's ticks, `drum-presets.ts` the devices' drum maps, `clock.ts` the audio clock against the page's, `fixtures.ts` the fakes the tests use instead of a device); `engine/midi.ts` (in `@yggi/cells`) is the pure part of MIDI out (a note as MIDI, the channel table, the rotation), `engine/midiclock.ts` (in `@yggi/cells`) that of the clock (the main meter, a window's ticks), `engine/midifile.ts` (in `@yggi/cells`) a MIDI file's bytes and back, `engine/midiexport.ts` (in `@yggi/cells`) a piece as one; `src/ui/state/midi.svelte.ts` is the `Midi` class in the `Studio` context, the player's `NoteSink`. Much of it is lifted from Jambot (`yggi/jambot` @ `8cdb6b8`); each lifted file says from where. MIDI learn: `src/ui/learn.ts` is the pure part (a message's knob, a `free()`'s place, pads, soft takeover, what is kept), `src/ui/state/learn.svelte.ts` the `Learn` class in the `Studio` (it hears the input through `Midi.hear`), `LearnButton.svelte` the control's button, `LearnSettings.svelte` its part of the page. MIDI in: `engine/keys.ts` (in `@yggi/cells`) is the pure part (which cells can play, a key through a melody's table, a cell's row as the note it plays, a kit's members by drum note, velocity as level), `src/ui/state/keys.svelte.ts` the `Keys` class in the `Studio` (the armed cell, the route from MIDI, the computer keys and the on-screen keys onto the player's `press`/`release`, the pedal, thru), `KeysSettings.svelte` its part of the page. Recording: `engine/capture.ts` (in `@yggi/cells`) is the pure part (a key as the number a cell writes, a take, the quantiser), `src/ui/state/record.svelte.ts` the `Recorder` in the `Studio` (it listens to `Keys` and holds a take), the record states in `src/audio/transport.ts`. The smoke test (`tools/smoke.mjs`) plays one piece into a fake port and checks the note-ons against the plan's notes, and the clock's ticks against the meter; plays a piece with MIDI cards (`midi-out`) and checks its note-ons are on the channels its cards name, and that its MIDI-only voice is sent and never sounds; then plays keys into a fake input (`window.midiIn(bytes)`) on a stopped piece and checks the armed voice sounds and stops, and thru; then records a pass of *Play in* from the fake input and checks the take held, kept and written.

# MIDI

MIDI on this device: the connection, the ports, All notes off, MIDI out (every voice heard sent to a port), MIDI clock, MIDI learn (a controller's knob moving a `free()`) and MIDI in (keys playing a voice live) (#203, the epic; this page grows with it).
What a device is, which knob moves what and which cell the keys play belong to this browser, not to the code. Which channel a voice goes to belongs to this browser too, unless the piece says it with MIDI cards ([../language/midi.md](../language/midi.md), below).

- **Where.** MIDI is a page of the sidebar (the ☰ drawer, whose tabs are *Controls*, *MIDI*, *Forces* and *Files*; `src/ui/Sidebar.svelte`, `src/ui/MidiPanel.svelte`). The *MIDI* button in the transport and the picker's *Files ▾* button (the Files page, beside it) open it; a dot on its tab says whether a port is connected. Its groups, top to bottom: the connection (Connect), *Devices* (the output, *No MIDI output* or a port; the input, *No MIDI input*, *All inputs* or a port; Refresh ports; All notes off), *Output slots this piece uses*, *This piece* (channels, the kit, its drum map, the built-in sound), *Clock and pitch*, *Keys*, *Learn*. Under them, in words, what was found or why there is no connection: no Web MIDI in this browser (Safari, Firefox without its add-on), or access blocked (a denied prompt, an iframe). Without MIDI everything else works as before.
- **Kept.** The ports chosen are kept in this browser by id and by name (`storageKey('cell-studio:midi')`). A port chosen before is connected again on load without a click, since a permission already granted needs no gesture; a first visit asks for nothing until Connect.
- **Unplugged and plugged in again.** Every change of the ports adopts the chosen ones again, since a port plugged in again is a new object: the output by its id, or, when that id is gone, a port with the same name (ids change on replug on some systems), and the input listened to anew. A chosen port that is absent shows as *Not connected*.
- **All notes off** sends CC 123 on every channel sent on since the last time (on all 16 when none was), and forgets them. It runs by itself when the tab comes back into view, since a tab frozen while hidden can leave a release unscheduled and hardware sounding.

## MIDI out

With an output chosen, every note the voices play goes to it too: the player hands each window's notes, on the audio clock, to its `NoteSink` beside the voices (`audio/player.ts`), so what is sent is what sounds, mutes and `off` included. A send (a voice played only into a resonator or a tape) is not heard from itself and is not sent.

- **When.** Each message is timestamped for the moment the built-in sound plays that note *heard*, not made (decision 005): `midi/clock.ts` reads the audio clock against `performance.now()` from the output timestamp's pairing (once the output has run 0.2 s; it follows a dropped buffer within three readings, and a leap of more than 50 ms, another device or a context resumed, at once), and the pictures' lag (`audio/heard.ts`, through `audio/device.ts` `heardLatency`) reads the same estimate. A timestamp is never in the past.
- **Channels.** The gates of a piece ([mix.md](../language/mix.md): a gate) and the voices it plays directly are numbered 1, 2, 3 … in the order it declares them, skipping the kit's channel (10), and wrap round to 1 after the fifteenth (a sixteenth voice shares channel 1). A gate is one part, all its receivers on its channel (`&leadOut trigger=&tune &lead &bell` sends both on one: layering is the synth's), so naming a gate is how a line is routed to gear by name; the page lists them and each can be put on another channel, kept per piece (by its title) in this browser. A kit's members (a kit read with `over=`, a groove's voices, a gate's kit) go to channel 10 (General MIDI's), or to the channel *Kit* is set to, kept per piece the same way (`kits` in the same key); the gates and voices then number round it, and a rotation leaves it out. MIDI in's on-screen pads and thru still read channel 10 for the kit, so they don't follow *Kit* ([#260](https://github.com/yggi/cell-studio/issues/260)).
- **A piece with MIDI cards** (`map=midi`, [../language/midi.md](../language/midi.md)) sets its channels itself: each note goes out on every channel its cards' entries put it on, and nothing else is sent (the default table above doesn't send the voices the cards leave out). The channel table lists the piece's channels as *set by the piece*, and they can't be changed there; rotation (MPE, below) is off for such a piece. A voice only on a MIDI card is sent and not heard: the player hands its notes to MIDI out marked `unheard`, and the built-in voices leave them out, whether the built-in sound is on or off.
- **Output slots.** A card says its output slot (`map=midi(10, 2)`; 1 when it says none): slot 1 is the output chosen above, and each other slot the open piece uses is listed in the page with a device to choose (*No device: not sent* until one is), kept per piece (by its title) in this browser (`slots` in the same key), by id and by name as the output is. Each slot sends through a `MidiOut` of its own, with the drum map kept for its device; All notes off, Stop and another device chosen silence it as they do the output. The clock and thru go to slot 1 only.
- **Drums.** A kit member's note is General MIDI, picked by its name: `bd`/`kick` 36, `rim` 37, `sd`/`snare` 38, `cp`/`clap` 39, `hh`/`ch` 42, `ph` 44, `oh` 46, `crash` 49, `ride` 51, `lt`/`lo` 41, `mt`/`mid`/`tom` 45, `ht`/`hi` 48 (a trailing number is ignored); any other name takes the next free note from 36. A device's own map is applied only at the output (`midi/drum-presets.ts`): *GM* sends them as they are, *Novation Circuit* onto its four drum notes 60, 62, 64 and 65, and a note typed in the page makes it *Custom*. The map is kept per output port.
- **Velocity** is the note's level at its start times the louder of its gains, as a square root (synths mostly turn velocity into amplitude on a square law), onto 1–127.
- **Pitch.** A note's frequency is the nearest key against A4 = 440 and the cents it is off it; a 12-EDO piece sends no bend at all. A microtonal note (`map=edo|ed(r)|ratios`) sends a bend before its note-on, and a note's glides go out as bends (a ramp every 20 ms), sent only up to the scheduling horizon. The bend range is a setting (±2 by default, ±48 for MPE synths). A bend is per channel, so notes with different bends that overlap on one channel sound out of tune: the page says so, and *A channel for each note (MPE)* rotates every voice's notes over a range of channels (2–16 by default, 10 left to a kit), each note on the channel free longest.
- **Note-offs.** Each channel and key remembers who holds it (start and end). A note-off goes only when nobody still holds that moment; a key struck again while held retriggers (off, then on).
- **Stop, a seek, All notes off.** A timestamped message can't be taken back (`MIDIOutput.clear()` isn't in Chrome), and notes go out up to the look-ahead (0.3 s, 0.12 s for pieces with hands) plus a window (0.25 s at the code's tempo) before they sound. So silencing sends All notes off at once on every channel in use, a note-off for every note still held (at once for one sounding, at its start for one already sent ahead, which All notes off now can't reach), and a bend back to the centre where one was left, after any bend sent ahead. Nothing later goes, so Play again, or a seek (a pause, then a start elsewhere), within the look-ahead isn't cut: where a note sent since sounds across a note-off left ahead, it is struck again just after it (a retrigger, not a cut).
- **Edits while playing.** A recompile (an edit, a mute, a held `free()` moved) swaps the plan at the next window and the routing with it; notes already held stay held and get their note-offs, as on the voices. Only a channel table that says something else (a voice put on another channel, a voice added) silences first, as Stop does; so does a change of bend range or rotation, and choosing another output silences the old one.
- **Built-in sound too.** Off, the built-in sound is muted at its output while MIDI plays on: the audio clock stays the master and the pictures still move. Hands (strike, bow) are not sent to MIDI, so with it off they are silent. The same goes for notes sounded by turning a piece's timeline by hand (`Player.scroll`): they go to the built-in voices, never to the MIDI output.
- **Unplugged while playing.** A send to a port that vanished is dropped (every send sits in a try); when it is plugged in again it is adopted, and the next notes go to it.

## MIDI clock

*Send MIDI clock* (off by default, kept per output port by its name) sends the output Timing Clock (0xF8) at 24 a beat, so a drum machine or a sequencer can follow the piece (#206).

- **Which beat.** The piece's main meter: the clock of its first played cell, followed up its `step=` chain to the meter with a rate of its own (sailor's `&bar`). A beat is one of its steps, or as many as `beat=` or `meter("4/4", 2)` say: `scan(4 4 4 4)` is four beats a bar, 96 ticks. A piece whose first cell steps at a bare rate with nothing stepping with it (`&tune step=4 0 2 4`, the telephone, the 5 to 12) has no meter and sends no clock; the page says so.
- **Laid through the meter's time map.** Tick k falls at the meter's step k·beat/24 through its time map (`engine/clocking.ts`), worked out from its index, never by adding up intervals (`engine/midiclock.ts`). So the ticks swing with `swing=`, bend with a scan (`scan(27 23 26 24)`), and speed up and slow down with a motor: spinning up, wow, running down; none go after a clock has come to rest or a piece has ended (`laps=`). A belted cell is not the meter; the meter it follows is.
- **When.** Each window's ticks go with its notes, in the same callback and through the same clock offset (`midi/clock.ts`), so they are timestamped for the heard moment as the notes are; never in the past, and never earlier than the clock message before them.
- **Start, Continue, Stop.** Start (0xFA) goes 2 ms before the first tick when play begins at the piece's start (from Stop, or paused at 0). Anywhere else (Play after Pause, a seek, the clock switched on while playing) the run begins on the next 16th, and a Song Position Pointer (0xF2, in 16ths from the piece's start) then Continue (0xFB) go before its first tick, so a device following picks up where the piece is. Stop (0xFC) goes on Pause, Stop, a seek and at a piece's end, and when the clock is switched off or another output is chosen. Ticks are sent ahead (the look-ahead plus a window) and can't be taken back, so Stop lands just after the last tick already sent, at most about half a second after now: no tick follows it. The next run waits until after that Stop, so Play again soon after Pause leaves the device stopped for that long while the piece plays on, then picks up in step.
- **A tempo moved while playing.** A held `free()` tempo, a hand on a motor or an edit lays the next window's ticks on the new grid. Where two grids meet, a tick nearer the last one than half its own spacing is left out, so none is doubled, and none is added. Moving the tempo also moves where the piece stands in beats (time is kept in seconds, so the beat at a moment changes, and the notes jump with it); a device following keeps counting from its own downbeat, so Pause and Play lines them up again.
- **A lossy grid.** 24 ticks a beat carry a 16th (6 ticks) and a triplet 8th (8), but not a quintuplet 16th (19.2) nor anything a bent scan puts between ticks (Jambot `docs/language-time.md` T3). The clock only counts time; the notes still go out where they are written.

## MIDI learn

A controller's knob, fader or pad moves a `free()` value exactly as its slider does ([../language/free.md](../language/free.md)); nothing is written into the piece.

- **Learn.** With MIDI connected and an input chosen, every `free()` control (the drawer and a cell's sheet; not a text) has *Learn*. Press it, then move a knob or press a pad: the next control change or note-on binds to it (Esc, or *Learn* again, cancels). A bound control shows its knob (*CC 7*, *note 36*, *CC 7 ch 2* off the first channel), and *Forget* (×) lets go. A knob learned for a second control leaves the first. The MIDI page says how many controls of the open piece are learned, with *Forget all*. A wait for a knob ends unbound when its control goes (its sheet closed), another piece is opened (or this one renamed), or the input goes; Esc is then everyone's again.
- **Moving.** CC 0–127 runs from `lo` to `hi` through the control's own scale (`src/ui/free.ts`, `freeAt`/`freePosition`, which the slider uses too): straight in the slider's steps (whole bpm for tempo), logarithmic for a frequency or a rate (a setting in Hz such as `lpf=`, `hpf=`, `tone=`, `wanderhz=`, which `src/engine/attributes.ts` marks; a clock's `step=` or `loop=` not in bpm) over a positive range of three octaves or more (`200..5000`: the middle is 1000), both ends exact; any other setting (`drive=`, `index=`) runs straight. The value then goes to `piece.hold`, as the slider's does, so it plays until kept and Stop lets go of it.
- **Choices.** A labelled `free()` (a keypad, a `meter()` choice) takes a knob in equal parts, one a choice; or pads: the pad learned picks the first choice, the pads above it on its channel the next ones (sixteen pads for a telephone keypad). On a slider, pads step from `lo` the same way.
- **Nothing jumps.** Soft takeover: a knob that isn't where the value is leaves it until it passes through it (or lands within a knob's step of it); until then the slider shows the gap, a band from the value to a mark where the knob is. Once something else moves the value (the slider, Stop, Keep, opening the piece again), the knob takes over afresh.
- **Cost.** Each hold compiles the piece again, so a fast knob holds at most once an animation frame per control: the last value before the frame. A tab that draws no frames (hidden, or behind another window) while MIDI keeps arriving holds within 50 ms instead.
- **Kept.** Bindings are this browser's (a controller is a device), kept by piece title and by the `free()`'s place as its cell and what it sets (`bar/step`, `fx/param:lpf`; a second of a kind in one cell `mix/gain#1`), not by its place in reading order, which an edit above it moves (`storageKey('cell-studio:midi-learn')`). A piece renamed (its title changed) starts with none; another browser has none. A renamed cell loses its bindings, and so does an unnamed one, `&( … )`, which is named by its line and column: a line inserted above it, or text before it on its line, moves it. They bind, they hold nothing, so Stop clearing what was held leaves them.
- **Echo out.** *Send a slider's moves back to its knob* in the MIDI page (off by default) sends a learned CC to the output when its slider or keypad is moved by hand, for controllers with motor faders or LED rings. A controller that sends it back moves the value to the knob's nearest of its 128 steps, so with echo on the slider is quantised to 128 steps (some bpm of a wide range can't be reached).

## MIDI in

A keyboard plays the piece's voices live, stopped or playing (#207). Nothing is written into the code; recording a braid left free is [below](#recording-a-braid-left-free).

- **Hands, not the plan.** A key is a hand on a voice, beside strike, damp and bow: `press(voice, freq, velocity)` gives an id and `release(id)` lets it go (`audio/player.ts`, `audio/voices.ts`, `audio/live.ts`). The note is held with the voice's own envelope until released, started at once (the context's `currentTime`, a few milliseconds on, not through the look-ahead; a key let go before that still sounds, then goes). A press takes a stopped transport to paused, as every hand does ([transport.md](transport.md)); a release with no press is ignored.
- **Pause and Stop.** Pause lets go of the plan's notes and leaves a key held sounding (it is a hand's, and the hand is still down); it goes into its release when the key comes up. Stop cuts it with everything else, and the keys forget what was down, so a key let go after Stop does nothing.
- **Which cell: the armed one.** *Play with* in the MIDI page chooses a cell, or leaves it to the cell whose sheet is open; with neither, the first cell that plays, in reading order (a melody, else a voice, before a kit). Arming is this page's state, not the code's. The voice armed and its melody light live as they sound (`ui/context.ts` `nodeState`), and the voice's wheel shows its sound.
- **The key through the tuning.** A melody (or a voice, through the first melody that plays it) sounds a key at the nearest pitch the melody can play: its numbers read through its table (`over=`), else its grid, down to the grid's tuning (`map=edo`, `ed(r)`, `ratios`), against the voice's own pitch and A4 (`engine/keys.ts`). Over a pentatonic, F sounds E and F♯ sounds G; over 19-EDO, a key takes the nearest of the 19 the table has; a key midway takes the lower. *A key outside the scale, played and recorded* is one setting for live play and takes alike (#210, [decision 018](../decisions/018-free-braid.md)): *Snap* (the default) as just said; *Allow* sounds every key at its own equal-tempered pitch (a take writes it with an accidental); *Ignore* sounds a key on the table at its pitch and one off it not at all. #207's *plays as played*, kept in this browser, reads as *Allow*, its *nearest* as *Snap*. The on-screen keys follow it too, since they go through the same `press`. A voice no melody plays sounds as played.
- **Kits.** Channel 10, or a kit armed, plays the kit's members by General MIDI drum note, the same notes MIDI out sends (`bd` 36, `sd` 38, `hh` 42 …). A device's note comes back through the output's drum map first (`midi/drum-presets.ts` `gmNotesFor`): under the *Novation Circuit* preset its pad 62 plays the first member that is a rim, snare, clap or tom.
- **Velocity** gives the level, on the square law MIDI out uses the other way (`keyLevel`): a key played in at velocity 90 and sent out again is sent at 90. **The sustain pedal** (CC 64, any channel) holds releases until it comes up; a key it holds struck again strikes again. **All notes off** and **All sound off** from the controller (CC 123 and CC 120, any channel) let every key go and the pedal up, so a lost note-off can't leave a note sounding; Stop does the same, and so do *All notes off* in the page, the page coming back into view (a key let go while it was hidden sent its note-off to no one), choosing another input, and the input listened to being unplugged (`Midi.letGo`).
- **Thru** (a setting, with an output chosen): every key and the pedal echoed to the output at once, without a timestamp, on the channel chosen, through `Midi.send` (so All notes off reaches it). Switching thru off, changing its channel, Stop and every let-go above first send a note-off for each key down (and the pedal up) on the channel they went out on, so the output isn't left holding them.
- **MIDI learn and the keys.** Both hear the input through `Midi.hear`, learn first. A message learn claims is not played (`Learn.claims`): any control change or note-on while a control waits for a knob, a CC learned, a pad learned or one above it within its control's choices. So CC 64 is the pedal unless it is learned for a slider (then it moves the slider only); forget it and it is the pedal again.
- **The computer keys** (*Play with the computer keys*, off by default, so the page keeps its keys): A to L the white keys and W E T Y U O the black ones from C4 to D5, 1 to 7 the kit (kick, snare, closed hat, open hat, pedal hat, rim, ride). A key typed into a field or the code editor (a contenteditable), a repeat or a chord with a modifier is left alone; a key down when the window loses focus lets go. The letters are read as typed (`KeyboardEvent.key`), so on a layout other than QWERTY they move with it (AZERTY's A is Q's place) and a Cyrillic layout plays none.
- **On-screen keys** (the dock's keyboard and pads, [keys.md](keys.md); the piano roll's key column, #211) go through the same path. A row of a cell: `studio.keys.pressRow(cell, value, velocity?)` and `studio.keys.releaseRow(cell, value)`, where `value` is a number written in the cell (an entry with laps), sounding as the cell plays it whatever is armed (`engine/keys.ts` `rowNote`: through its tables to its grid's tuning, on its out's voice; a kit's row is its member); `studio.keys.heldRows(cell)` (reactive) is the rows down, the pedal's included. A plain keyboard: `studio.keys.press(note, velocity?, channel?)`, `release(note, channel?)` and `held` (the notes down, any channel but 10). Both take the pedal, strike again when pressed again, and go out through thru (a row as the MIDI note nearest).
- **Latency.** In headless Chromium a live note reaches the voice's analyser about 7 ms after the message (the smoke test's median of five), and the output adds its own (42 ms there, `baseLatency + outputLatency`). On a phone it is #154's measurement; `balanced` against `interactive` (#91) matters most here.

## Recording a braid left free

A take records the keys into a braid left free, `free(0 2 4 ~ …)` ([../language/free.md](../language/free.md#a-braid-left-free), [decision 018](../decisions/018-free-braid.md), #210). It is held, not written, until Keep.

- **What it records into.** The armed cell, when its braid is left free and it plays notes on a voice (not a kit); with a voice armed, the first such cell that plays it. **●** (record) is on the transport for a piece with a braid left free, greyed out while nothing is armed that it can record into.
- **One pass.** ● arms a take and starts play. The next whole pass of the cell's loop is taken (a pass just begun, within half a step, counts): ● blinks while it waits and is lit while it takes. ● again, Pause or Stop calls it off and holds nothing.
- **When a key went down.** Each key carries its event's `timeStamp` (a MIDI message's, a key event's; the on-screen keys take the moment they are pressed). It goes through the clock offset (`midi/clock.ts`, the one MIDI out's timestamps use) to the moment heard on the audio clock, and through the transport to where the piece was heard then (`Player.heardAt`). That is mapped to the cell's step through its time map (`engine/clocking.ts`), so a swung, bent or motor-driven clock is undone. Positions stay exact until the pass is over; then each key starts on its nearest step (half a step rounds later), and a key starting outside the pass is dropped.
- **What is written** (`engine/capture.ts`). A key is the number the cell writes for its pitch, through its table and tuning, under the setting above: *Snap* the nearest entry, *Allow* the nearest step of the grid below as an entry with accidentals (`3#`: the fewest, sharps before flats), *Ignore* nothing for a key off the table. A row of the cell's piano roll is its own number. Keys starting on one step are a chord `[0,4]` as long as the longest, a key held over steps `4@2` (no further than the next key), a step nothing covers `~`. The sustain pedal sounds on, but a note ends at its key's release.
- **Held.** At the pass's end the take is held in the braid's place (one recompile a pass), shown in the cell's sheet with **Keep** and **Revert**, and the transport's *Save* and *Revert* take it with the rest of what is held. Another take replaces it; Stop lets go of it.

## The code

`src/midi/` is the browser glue (`access.ts` the Web MIDI connection, `routing.ts` adopting the ports, `surfaces.ts` reading a message, `out.ts` sending the notes played, `clock-out.ts` sending the clock's ticks, `drum-presets.ts` the devices' drum maps, `clock.ts` the audio clock against the page's, `fixtures.ts` the fakes the tests use instead of a device); `src/engine/midi.ts` is the pure part of MIDI out (a note as MIDI, the channel table, the rotation), `src/engine/midiclock.ts` that of the clock (the main meter, a window's ticks); `src/ui/state/midi.svelte.ts` is the `Midi` class in the `Studio` context, the player's `NoteSink`. Much of it is lifted from Jambot (`yggi/jambot` @ `8cdb6b8`); each lifted file says from where. MIDI learn: `src/ui/learn.ts` is the pure part (a message's knob, a `free()`'s place, pads, soft takeover, what is kept), `src/ui/state/learn.svelte.ts` the `Learn` class in the `Studio` (it hears the input through `Midi.hear`), `LearnButton.svelte` the control's button, `LearnSettings.svelte` its part of the page. MIDI in: `src/engine/keys.ts` is the pure part (which cells can play, a key through a melody's table, a cell's row as the note it plays, a kit's members by drum note, velocity as level), `src/ui/state/keys.svelte.ts` the `Keys` class in the `Studio` (the armed cell, the route from MIDI, the computer keys and the on-screen keys onto the player's `press`/`release`, the pedal, thru), `KeysSettings.svelte` its part of the page. Recording: `src/engine/capture.ts` is the pure part (a key as the number a cell writes, a take, the quantiser), `src/ui/state/record.svelte.ts` the `Recorder` in the `Studio` (it listens to `Keys` and holds a take), the record states in `src/audio/transport.ts`. The smoke test (`tools/smoke.mjs`) plays one piece into a fake port and checks the note-ons against the plan's notes, and the clock's ticks against the meter; plays a piece with MIDI cards (`midi-out`) and checks its note-ons are on the channels its cards name, and that its MIDI-only voice is sent and never sounds; then plays keys into a fake input (`window.midiIn(bytes)`) on a stopped piece and checks the armed voice sounds and stops, and thru; then records a pass of *Play in* from the fake input and checks the take held, kept and written.

# The graph

How a piece is drawn, arranged and touched. Editing a cell is on [editor.md](editor.md).

The code is the single source of truth. The graph draws exactly the cells and links written (the implicit default grid is not drawn); edges come from the references in the code. Every change made in the graph is a rewrite of the cell's own line (`src/engine/edit.ts`), so the two views stay 1:1.

## Drawing

- **Braids** are drawn as what they play, coloured through the cell's own table, on a **track** ([decision 014](../decisions/014-splices-are-bulbs.md), `src/layout/track.ts`): the ring and its splices are one smooth closed curve. Each `&ref` bends out of the ring into a **bulb**, its own steps round it in time, on a thin stem that takes no time; a splice in a splice is a bulb on its bulb, so `&leadA` (made of `&m1 &m2`) wears the motifs' colours, each on its own bulb. A repeat (`&x!2`) goes round its bulb that many times (`×2` at its hub). The hand bends from the hub through each bulb's stem and hub to the step playing now. A cell spliced into one braid only, and read nowhere else, is drawn there and has no node of its own (`GraphModel.bulbs`: its edges are drawn from the braid, as a petal's); a cell read elsewhere too is a **net-ref**: its bulb outlined dotted, its edge coming in through the reader's loom to the outside of each bulb that shows it. The layout leaves a braid the room its bulbs take, and its name goes under them.
- **Meters are ground**, as in a circuit: the edges into a meter (`step=&bar`, and a parameter that takes a clock: `delaytime=&bar*3/4`, a tape's `length=`, a head's `at=`) aren't drawn. A meter's wheel stays put as it plays; its step now goes round, outlined and glowing in its colour. Each meter's name is in its colour (the first: the accent; `--meter-*` in `app.css`). A step between two turntables is a belt, drawn: a spring when the driven cell says `belt=`, rigid otherwise (a tape on its motor's capstan); a belt is drawn over the wheels and counts as two crossings for each it makes. The layout doesn't pull on those edges either (`src/layout/meters.ts`). Instead every cell a meter steps, or that takes a time from it, is joined to it by its **net**, like ground on a circuit board: not an edge each, but a tree of straight traces through the meter's other cells (a spanning tree, shortest first, each costed by the edges it crosses and the wheels it passes; `meterNet`). A trace is a backbone, a wide line in the meter's colour; it carries no pulse, but every trace of a meter blinks at once on each of its steps (brighter on its first, and in the tick's level token: `--meter-0` the bar's first, `--meter-1` a beat, finer levels of a `meter(…)` `--meter-2` … 3), one signal (`MeterEdge.svelte`). A meter is laid out with the rest: its net's tree holds it and its cells by springs that aren't drawn, so it settles among them; once the layout has settled it is placed last, where its net costs least (crossing nothing it can avoid, passing through no wheel), and held there; then its net is laid (`layNet` in `src/layout/physics.ts`).
- **A table of taps is a wheel of sub-wheels** ([decision 016](../decisions/016-arranging-at-the-input.md)): `&parts step=&bar &keysBar &drumBar` draws each tap as a bulb on `&parts`' track, as a splice is ([decision 014](../decisions/014-splices-are-bulbs.md)), the table itself shaped as a bulb: its taps fanned over the top two thirds of its wheel in braid order, left to right, the bottom third left for its inputs (the meter's bus, and the selector's read: one plain wire, no loom; `fanOf` in `layout/track.ts`). Each tap is a closed ring in its part's colour on the selector's barrel (`partColour`), named by its cell less what all of them end in; playing, the selector's cursor points into each tap it has on, in its colour, and the others are dimmed; the meter's bus comes into the table once, and each tap's **sub-net** (a net of its own, in the table's colour, flashing on the tap's ticks) leaves from its bulb to the cells it steps (`GraphEdge.bulb`, `bulbAt`; `meterNet` lays one per tap). **Any bulb is its cell**: tapped, it opens that cell's sheet; held, it switches it off and is dimmed (a tap without a line of its own gets one, `&keysBar off`).
- **A gate is a wheel** (#234, [mix.md](../language/mix.md): a gate): a product with a name (`trigger=`) draws its trigger's notes round its ring, its hand where the trigger plays; `trigger` and `velocity` come in as wires of their own, and its voices sit as small wheels over its top (`layout/gate.ts`), lit while they sound. A voice only the gate plays is one of its bulbs (tap to open, hold to switch off); one played elsewhere too keeps its node and is drawn hollow here. Its products have no junction dots: the gate is their junction.
- **A product is a junction** ([nets.md](../notes/nets.md), decision 2): `[&theme * &flute * .4]` is a small dot of its own, as `&( … )` is a cell of its own. What is written in it goes in (the melody, levels, pitches, an envelope), the voice is its definition (a dotted `is`, mostly under the voice's wheel), and its sound goes out to the mix with its gain. So a voice is no hub: each melody's sound has its own wire to its own mix. The dot sits on its voice's rim, facing what goes into it and off the voice's name; at rest it turns to where its wires cross least (`src/layout/junctions.ts`). It weighs nothing in the layout, which lays the graph with each junction folded into its voice. It isn't a cell: tapping, dragging, holding or deleting it does nothing, and it is lit while its voice plays into its mix.
- **Labels** on an edge say what is written where the name is read, as written: how many times (`×2`), the attribute it feeds (`fm`, `lpf`, `write`, `play`, `map`: a wave's table, [nets.md](../notes/nets.md)), and the transform on the wire — a pick or shift (`[::-1]`, `[<<2]`), `^1` (degrees), `inverse`, `^$7` (grid steps), a number in a pitch sum (`−7`), a gain into a mix (`×0.4`), a rate (`÷2`, `⟳`, `loop`, `· belt`), a read head's `at=` — then how its port combines it (`×` an envelope or a level, `+`/`−` a pitch). One pure function draws them from the wire (`edgeLabel`, `src/engine/transform.ts`); more than three different transforms end in `…`.
- **Nets** (`GraphModel.nets`): a cell read by several through one port is one net, laid as a tree whose junctions are its readers (`netTree`). A table's or a melody's leaves it once, to the reader nearest, and goes on from reader to reader, flared out of each wheel; sound (a voice into several mixes) branches from its owner to each. So does a net read at delays (`delayed`): a canon's copies (`&john[<<1]`, its voices or the cells it is spliced into) and a tape's heads (`at=`) each leave the owner with the delay on the tap, in the code's own spelling (`[<<2]` in steps, `at=.375` in seconds), as a tape's heads leave the tape, since each delay counts from the owner ([decision 010](../decisions/010-canon-copies.md)); their springs are the edges' own. The layout's springs follow the tree, laid again every tick, so a moved cell is joined where it's nearest, as a ratsnest.
- **Voices, mixes and the sound card** are wheels that don't spin: a spectral waterfall. Left channel on the left half, right on the right; 20 Hz meets at the bottom, 20 kHz at the top (log scale); the newest frame at the hub, flowing outward. Colour is the note of each band on the grid the wheel follows ([theme.md](theme.md): the palette's sound) and brightness is level. A voice follows its notes' grid (a rainbow per period from its pitch 0). A mix or the sound card follows the one grid all its voices are on (from C4: on the usual grid, C red); voices on several grids (a 12-tone and a 19-tone voice) collide and it shows no colour, a neutral waterfall, unless its own `show=mix:&edo19` names the grid to follow. A voice with no pitched notes (a kit, a gate) has no say (`heardGrid`, `src/layout/show.ts`). At rest a voice shows its own partials at pitch 0, lifted so they read, inside a rim.
- Roots sit at the centre; cells ring outward along the tables they read through. Tables (roots, scales, meters) are drawn as their entries on the grid below; melodies as slots in time.
- **A wheel turns; its taps stay** (`cellView`, `CellGlyph`): its inner ring turns with `shift=` (a braid's steps, a table's entries), the entry now first under a marker fixed at 12 o'clock, outlined. A table's **taps** sit on a ring just outside it (`wheelGeometry().rTap`), one per entry, drawn whether anything reads them or not: they ride their entries, spaced by pitch (the default, the table's pattern), or stay put, evenly round (`show=equal`). A table that takes some of the slots of the table below (a mode of its key, a scale of the 12) shows what comes in at its **hub** — the slots as the table below pushes them, the ones it takes lit — and the **gear**, a wire from each slot taken out to its entry; they turn with the ring. The sheet draws the same wheel, larger, and edits on it.
- **The loom** (`loomPaths`): a wheel's strands leave their taps along the radius onto tracks of their own round it and off towards their reader through a short curve, nested so none cross — a ring round the source; from there the bus keeps its width, its strands side by side along one curve (`src/layout/wire.ts`). Each bus from a wheel takes a band of its one loom (`loomRings`, the larger inside; a table read by several leaves it once). A strand carries what its source pushes (its entry, turned as it stands), whatever the reader takes of it. A pick (`&key[0]`) is a strand from its tap, straight out; several from one reader are a bus; a `$` pick is a wire from the hub with its label ([decision 012](../decisions/012-picks-are-taps.md)).
- While playing, every table a note passes through lights the entry it used.
- **The tower** (the *tower* button; [multiscale.md](../notes/multiscale.md), [tower-rows.md](../notes/tower-rows.md)): a logarithmic axis of time at the graph's right edge, from the constant (a table nothing steps) at the bottom through the slow (under 0.5 Hz), the pulse and the audible (from 20 Hz) to the sound card on top, its decades labelled as periods below 1 Hz and as frequencies above. Each cell is a mark at its height: a clocked cell a bar from its turn (how often it comes round) up to its step (how often it moves), so nesting shows; a voice at its pitch, in its colour; a mix just above the sound it carries (`towerSpans`, `src/layout/tower.ts`). The axis sits beside the graph (the graph is framed again in what is left, so it hides no cell), and while a cell's card is open on a wide screen it stands at the card's left edge and the graph is framed again in the room left (with less than 240 px of graph left, the tower waits for the card to close), so the selected cell's line and name stay in sight and no cell is covered. The layout stays the signal path's: the axis, not a cell's height in the graph, says its time. Limits: a table with no clock of its own (a table of taps, `&parts`; a kit, `&clicks`) sits at the constant, though what reads it moves it; on a phone the strip takes the same width (160 px), and a cell's card covers it.
- **Edges have no colours of their own**: at rest they are the neutral `--edge`; live, each takes the colour of what it carries now, by the palette ([theme.md](theme.md)): a melody or a ref the note under the playhead on its grid, a level (an envelope, a gain, `lpf=`) the neutral hue as bright as the level, a step the level of the tick (dotted, `--meter-0` on the bar's first), sound the hue of its fundamental (a mix's sound carries several: neutral). The pulse dot is its edge's colour. A belt is the clock's tick, brighter as it pulls; a meter's net is its meter's colour, flashing to the tick's level on each step; an `about=` link is never live.
- The playhead is white with a dark edge on every wheel, against the colours of what is live. An envelope (`show=adsr`) names its values on its curve from 112 px up (A 3 ms, D 180 ms, S 10 %, R 60 ms); in a braid's sheet a written number shows to three figures.
- A played braid's moving shift is its playhead; a restarted part's playhead shows the global clock (see [../limits.md](../limits.md)).

## States

Every node and edge is drawn in one of five states, decided for both by one model (`src/engine/routing.ts`, so a node and its edges can't disagree):

- **off**: switched off (`off` in the code; hold to toggle). Faded, grey, a dashed ring; its edges faded.
- **unrouted**: on, but not part of anything that sounds. Faded, once the transport is not stopped.
- **on** (stopped: nothing is dimmed, routed or not), **idle** (paused or playing, not sounding at that moment: quieter, less colour) and **live** (sounding at that moment: full colour; a live edge glows, pulses and takes its note's colour).

The **heads** are drawn in every state: the playheads, the lit entries, the beat of a meter, the tables turned, all at the transport's time (`Playback.heads`, the live state at that time): time 0 stopped, the place paused, the clock playing. What the sound shows follows the transport: stopped, nothing sounds, so an instrument shows its default waveform and the sound card and spectrum wheels their partials. Paused or playing, a mix, a read head and the sound card show what they carry (a flat line while nothing sounds in them), and an instrument shows its live waveform while it is routed, its default while it is not (faded); a note's colour on an edge and a scope follow the same. A meter's flash is a tick, so it waits for playing.

An output's route is what its notes need (its braid and tables, the `step=` chain and clock that move it, what moves its voice: pitch and volume streams, the envelope, parameter cells, FM) and where its sound goes (voice, instruments, mix, the sound card); a part is on the route while its selector has its tap on (the `step=` chain through the tap). Sound tapes and resonators keep sound and pass it on wherever their heads and mixes go. Something is routed if it is on the route of an output that can sound (`plan`'s own test) and reaches a sound card; live if it is on the route of one that plays now (until its `laps=` end, while its selectors pick it). Inside a braid only the cell and table entries under the playhead are live. Paused, that is where the transport stands. A voice in two mixes lights only the mix its notes take now.

*Live* means its part plays now, not that a note sounds this instant: a rest or a missed chance in a part that plays stays live (the glyphs show the step). The spectrum wheels show the sound itself; a mix's wheel hears the notes that go through it (with a limit: [../limits.md](../limits.md)).

## Arrangement

A layered drawing, the textbook way: rows by signal flow (the sound card on top, voices, melodies, the parts and tables they read — tuning a row under content; a meter has no row of its own), each row ordered to cross as few edges as it can (an edge to the meter counts a quarter: it says when, not what), each node placed towards its neighbours (`src/layout/layered.ts`). Tags inside a piece (two cells or more) are laid out first as blocks of their own and the piece around them, so a tag starts in one place instead of spread over every row.

The physics only relaxes it — a spring to each node's place, no overlaps — and nothing is pinned. From that start, balanced forces give each graph its own shape: edges contract, nodes repel and leave room for labels, the edges at a node spread round it (stars and mirror symmetries emerge), meters and sound keep apart, tagged cells cluster (and a hub's pull on a cluster — the meter, a selector, a mix — moves the cluster as one, mostly); crowded rows become tracks (a cell's parts in a column under it). A tape's heads face what they connect to: their spacing round the wheel is fixed (each read head further round from the write head by how far along the tape it reads), the wheel itself turns so the heads face their cells best (busy cells count more), and what each head plays or records sits towards it (`src/layout/tape.ts`).

## Touch

- **Tap** a cell: a detail sheet to turn it (drag the rim, − / +: writes `shift=`), switch it on/off (writes `off`), pin it in the layout, or jump to its line in the code (*Show in code*).
- **Hold**: quick toggle of `off`.
- **Drag**: moves the cell (physics keeps the rest arranged). Drag a hub to move, a rim to shift; double-tap pins.
- **Keyboard**: in a sheet the arrows turn the rim; stopped, a focused plugin dial turns by its own step (the sun, an hour) and a turntable by a sixteenth (Enter or Space opens a turntable).
- Pinning is layout, not music, so it isn't in the code (yet).

On a phone, **Graph** and **Code** are two full-size views switched by tabs under the header (the Code tab shows a count when there are errors; *Show in code* switches to it and selects the line). Both views stay mounted, so the graph keeps its layout while you edit. On a wide screen they sit side by side. See [theme.md](theme.md).

# Changelog

What changed in Cell studio, newest first. The app is deployed from `main` on every push, so
this lists user-visible changes; each release (a version bump in `package.json` and a `vX.Y.Z`
tag) gathers the entries under *Unreleased* beneath its own heading. The version and commit of
the running build are shown in the piece picker's **Files** menu.

Format: [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/). Add a line to *Unreleased* in the PR
that makes the change. Also note changes to the language that could affect saved `.cells` pieces.

## Unreleased

### Added
- `degree(&x, n)` and `inverse(&x)` in a braid: a motif moved n degrees up the table it is read through, or mirrored within it, without a cell of its own reading a shifted table (#31). They nest: `degree(inverse(&x), 4)`. Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected (both were errors before).

- In a pitch sum, of two terms with `step=` timing the finer one starts the notes and the coarser one holds under it (a progression: `&changes step=&bar*4 0 0 5 0` beside `&walk step=&bar`), as a `loop=` term would; before, this was an error and a progression had to be written `loop=&bar*N`. Two at the same rate are still an error. Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.

- A stepped braid of voices plays them: `&groove step=&bar/4 &bd ~ &hh [&bd,&sd] &hh*2` is a drum pattern by name, with no kit and no numbers to look up; it plays what the same pattern read through a kit plays. An unstepped braid of voices it splices is a drum motif (`&fill &sd &sd*2 ~ &hh`, spliced, reversed, nested). Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected (it was an error before).

- `pan=` on a voice places it in stereo: 0 left, .5 centre (as without it), 1 right, the far side fading; one chord with panned voices plays what two chords with different gains play. **Saved pieces:** `pan=` was an error since the start (it said "balance with two mixes"); two chords keep working ([decision 007](docs/decisions/007-pan-is-back.md)).

- A turning table (a stepped key, a scale stepped a degree at a time, a braid of chords) comes round after its own length without `cycle=`; `cycle=` is needed only to say otherwise (`cycle=24`: two laps). **Saved pieces:** a table turned without `cycle=` used to climb a lap each time round and now comes round instead; give it a larger `cycle=` to keep climbing ([decision 008](docs/decisions/008-tables-come-round.md)). Every example plays the same; the piece lengths the app shows follow `cycle=` too (IDM 3:30 → 0:30, Per tonos 1:40 → 3:20, and the Ricercar's cycle doubles, which its 30 laps hide).

- The examples are rewritten in the new forms and play the same notes: `degree(…)` instead of a cell reading a shifted table (Twinkle, Lullaby, Five, Pop, Drunken Sailor, Zelluläre Aktivität, Cell Tines), drums by name (Drunken Sailor, IDM, Cellular Brothers, Automaton), one chord and `pan=` instead of two (Two Tuning Forks, Kalimba, Cell Phase, Tape Machine), progressions stepped a bar at a time (Swing blues, Five, Hypercell, One more Cell), the Prelude's chords as a braid of chords, and no `loop=SAMPLE_RATE` or `cycle=` where they said the default. Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- Stereo by `pan=` in Automaton, Cell in Focus, Ghost-Cells, Music Box, the Ricercar, Study after Studie II, Cell Tines and Cellscape: one chord on the sound card instead of two, each voice placed with its pan rounded to two places, so a quiet side moves by 0.25 dB at most (inaudible). Cellscape drops its two selectors and twelve per-ear mixes for one of each, and plays each note once with both channels' gains instead of once per ear. *Prelude and Fugue in C minor* (BWV 847) is written by its generator in the new forms: the prelude's hands read braids of four-note chords instead of strided tables, and its stereo is `pan=` (two pans rounded: 0.09 dB at most). The tresillo in *Meter and rhythm* steps from the bar (`step=&bar*4/3`), so it keeps time when the tempo slider moves (it drifted before).
- Each PR's build is published as a preview at `…/cell-studio/pr-<n>/` (the PR's comment says where), to try a change on a phone before it's merged; it goes when the PR closes. A preview keeps its own settings (`?latency=`, `?lookahead=`, `?perf`, the engine, the library's layout), so trying one doesn't change the app's, and its own saved pieces (open yours there as a `.cells` file). The app keeps the settings it had. Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.

### Fixed
- A piece's loop length counts a turning table's turns where they really wrap: IDM: drill loops in 0:30 (its harmony turns twice and back), not 3:30; Canon per tonos in 3:20 (twelve passes, two octaves, home), not 1:40 (six, an octave up). Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- Switching off a mix or the sound card silences the tape heads that return through it, and a resonator ringing alone in it, as the graph already showed (#165): in *Sound on Tape*, muting `&out` used to leave the heads playing. Nothing changes while everything is on. Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- A mix with thousands of nested parentheses, or a chain of thousands of cells spliced into each other, no longer overflows the stack: each is one error (parentheses nest 32 deep, splices 256), and a failed sound-card line no longer adds "Nothing plays yet" (#160, #163). A cap names an unnamed cell `&( … )` as such, not by its generated name. An `anchor=` left on a law no longer moves where *5 to 12* starts (the clock's own `anchor=` does, as the law reads it). Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected, unless one nests that deep.
- What played nothing, or the wrong thing, without a word now says so (#143): a cell that would stand for more than 50,000 notes (`&a!4000` over `&a 0!4000`) or start more than 2,000 notes a second (`step=1000000`) is an error that names the cap (the player keeps the last piece that compiled); unnamed cells `&( … )` nest 32 deep at most (deeper crashed the compiler); in `over=scale(tonic, phase)` a phase beyond 0–6 is an error and a tonic beyond an octave a warning; a label field that isn't one (`{lap}`, `{tempi}`) is a warning that lists the fields; `anchor=` on a law is a warning that it belongs on the clock the law reads (`over=&day`); a cell whose line failed is no longer reported again as an unknown cell (or a product with nothing to play) everywhere it's used. **Saved pieces:** one that goes over a cap or writes `scale(t, 7)` and beyond now shows an error instead of playing nothing; an `anchor=` left on a law, or an unknown label field, now warns. No example is affected.
- Opening a piece whose graph has a chord or a table bus no longer throws while the previous piece gives way (Baustelle, Baustelle II failed the smoke test). Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- A read head of a sound tape (`play=` on a tape that records a mix) showed an empty wheel: it shows the waveform of what it plays back, with where it reads (`0.75s`). Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.

### Changed
- Graph: an edge's label says the whole transform written where the name is read: a tape head's `at=` (`play at=0.375`), a ref's pick or shift (`[::-1] ^8`, `[<<] ^-12`), a number in a pitch sum (`[<<] −7`); more than three different transforms on one edge end in `…` (#146). Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- A mix's wheel hears only the notes that go through it (#77): a voice in two mixes shows in each only while its notes go through that one (in *Baustelle*, `&einsturz` stays flat until it is picked at the end, where it used to move all piece long). A mix also hears what goes through its mixes and instruments, and a drum kit's stream in it (#166); its voices show at the levels they are heard at. The sound is unchanged. Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- Graph: a canon's voices are taps on its leader's net, each straight from the leader with its delay on it (`[<<2]`), as a tape's heads come off the tape; before, they hopped from voice to voice, so `[<<3]` seemed to count from the voice before. *Frère Jacques* and *Per tonos* change. `[<<n]` stays a copy, the score replayed n steps later ([decision 010](docs/decisions/010-canon-copies.md), #147). Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- A mix that is switched off (or whose voices are) shows nothing on its wheel: the mix's waveform listens only to what reaches it, as the sound does. The sound itself is unchanged: where it goes is now wired from the same description the graph draws (#132). Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- Dough's other names for sound parameters (`sound` `cutoff` `resonance` `hcutoff` `hresonance` `verb` `reverb` `delayt` `delayfb`) are no longer read: each is an error that names ours (`cutoff=` → `lpf=`, `verb=` → `room=` …). **Saved pieces** that use one stop compiling until it is renamed; no example does.
- Graph: a piece opens laid out from the better of two starts — its rows ordered by the sweeps, or filled one cell at a time from the sound card — tried side by side: the library's crossings fall by a quarter. An example's order is chosen when the app is built, so it opens as fast as before; a piece of your own takes up to a second longer to open. A net's joins now keep off the wheels between them (they never did: a bug). Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- Graph: sound branches from the voice to each mix (a star); a meter is placed last again, where its net crosses least, once the layout has settled: the library's crossings fall by another sixth. Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- Graph: a net runs from reader to reader. A table, a melody or a voice read by several leaves once and goes on from one reader to the next, which is its junction, instead of branching in mid-air; the layout's springs follow each net's tree, and the library's crossings fall by a quarter. A table's strands leave their wheel without crossing; a ref written more than once is drawn as a bus. Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- The sun splits in two: a day is a meter (`&day scan(1!24) loop=1d anchor=11.55`, with the new time units `min` `h` `d` `wk`), drawn as the 24-hour dial with `show=sun`; a law reads it with `over=&day`; `show=brightness` shades a mode table's segments by brightness. **Saved pieces:** `sun=` is retired (it warns as unknown) and `anchor=` on the law itself no longer moves it; move `anchor=` to a day meter and point the law at it with `over=` (an `anchor=` left on a law is ignored, with a warning). *5 to 12* is rewritten and sounds the same.
- The graph's layout weighs its cells: a turntable heaviest, then a meter, a table read by several, a sound, a cell, and the heavier holds more room round it. Over the library: 83 crossings (89 before). Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- A table read by several cells is a net, laid as a ratsnest: one bundle of strands leaves its wheel for the nearest reader, and each other reader branches off it where it's nearest (a T), laid again as cells move, so a moved cell takes the best place to attach. A wheel's strands leave it as curves that keep their order, from the far side diving under the wheel, so none cross where they leave. Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- A meter's net is a backbone: a wide trace in the meter's colour, lit on the beat (it was a thin line that the edges' own width rule overrode). Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- Kalimba reads through its own tines: `&tune over=&kalimba`, and `&notes` (the same eight numbers written twice) is gone; each note plucks the tine at its place. It plays the same notes. In the graph the tune and the kalimba share one bus, from its pads. Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- A meter is no longer placed last, apart from the rest: it is laid out with the cells it steps (held to them by springs that aren't drawn), and its net is laid after. Over the library that's 13 more crossings (89): fewer in the Prelude, Zelluläre Aktivität and Korobeiniki, more in Cellscape, Hypercell and the Lullaby. Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- A wheel's rim says what space it is drawn in: a table, scale, key or grid (laid out by pitch) wears the grid as a rainbow rim, and its wedges are bright for the step they hold and shaded over the steps the table leaves out, so a scale reads as a sieve of the circle; a melody (laid out by time) wears a clock, a tick a step. Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- `show=wave` is the default for a voice, a mix and a read head (`show=spectrum` is for the sound card). Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- Voices show their waveform (one cycle; while playing, the sound itself, held still) instead of a spectrum; mixes show their waveform too (a flat line until they play, captioned with their voices); only the sound card shows a spectrum. `show=spectrum` on a voice brings its spectrum back. A table's strands run as a cable loom: out of each entry at a right angle, round the wheel on a lane each, into a bundle towards the reader. Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- A cell stepped by a meter no longer wears the meter's ring (with its rate): the meter's net says which meter steps it. A step between two turntables is drawn as a belt even without `belt=` (a tape on its motor's capstan: rigid). Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- The graph draws an edge by what it carries ([notes/nets.md](docs/notes/nets.md)), from what is read to the cell that reads it: a clock's ticks dotted, with the pulse running from the driver; sound as a wave (a voice into a mix, FM, a resonator excited, a tape of sound); values solid. A table read through `over=` is a bus, a strand per entry in the colours of its wheel (a scale is seven strands from its wheel to the theme's), with the entries looked up now lit while it plays; an edge carrying chords is a strand per note, in the colours of the notes sounding. A tune into a resonator with tines (the Kalimba) reaches it as a strand per tine, ending at its pad and lit while it rings. A cell spliced into another's braid meets its wheel at the segment where it sits, once for each time it is written, and a table's strands leave its wheel from their entries. A belt counts as drawn (over the wheels, both runs), and a meter with a belt is placed where its belt crosses least. Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- `docs/examples.md` is generated from `examples/*.cells` (#55): title, file, tags, description, level and length of every piece, grouped by genre in the library's order; `npm run docs:examples` rewrites it and a test fails when it is stale. Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- The graph joins each meter to every cell it steps by a net (#27), like ground on a circuit board: not an edge each, but thin solid traces in the meter's colour through its other cells, laid to cross as little as possible; they carry no pulse but all blink together on the meter's steps. A meter is placed last, once the rest has settled, where its net is shortest and crosses least (Royal theme's meter sits inside its ring; it no longer sits alone at the bottom). A cell's meter ring now goes round at the cell's rate (twice as fast at ×2, half at ÷2). Over the examples every one of the 214 meter-stepped cells is joined, at about 12 more crossings, in about 7 % less room. Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- The library's miniatures match the graph (#27): a meter's edges aren't drawn there either, and the frame holds every node whole (the top and bottom wheels were cut off). Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- The graph draws meters as ground (#27): the edges into a meter (`step=&bar`) are gone; each cell a meter steps wears a ring in that meter's colour, made of the meter's own steps where they are on the meter, the current one bright (beside the cell's own current step when the meter drives it 1:1), with its rate on it (`÷2`, `loop ×16`); a meter's current step is outlined and glowing in its colour; each meter's name is in its colour (pieces with two meters, like Bossa nova or Meter and rhythm, show which cells follow which). Without those edges the graphs cross far less (41 crossings over the examples instead of 121) and are a little larger. A belted cell keeps its belt. Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- Dialogs hold the focus (#9): a cell's sheet, the step and setting editors and a piece's card in the library take the focus when they open, Tab goes round their own controls, Escape closes only the top one (Escape in a step editor no longer closes the cell's sheet under it too), and the focus goes back where it was when they close. Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- A pitch drop is a pitch shape (#30): `pitch=0..30 * &(map=adsr 0 .04 0 0)`, a range in semitones scaled by an envelope in seconds, written like the moving parameters (`lpf=110..3800 * &fenv`). It replaces `penv=30 pdec=.04`, which still plays, with a warning naming the new form. The 16 examples that used it (19 voices: kicks, the Metronome's clicks, Baustelle's basses) are rewritten and render the same (golden renders, all 52 pieces). Saved `.cells` pieces play as before.
- `penv=` and `pdec=` are gone (#30): a pitch drop is written only as a pitch shape, `pitch=0..30 * &(map=adsr 0 .04 0 0)`. **Saved `.cells` pieces** that use them no longer play: the error names the replacement (`penv=30 pdec=.04` is `pitch=0..30 * &(map=adsr 0 .04 0 0)`; `pdec=` was .05 when left out). No example uses them.
- The graph keeps a piece's tags apart (#27): each tag (two cells or more) is laid out first as a block of its own, then the piece around it, and the hubs every part meets (the meter, a selector, a mix) pull a cluster as one instead of pulling its cells into the other clusters. Over the examples, overlapping clusters 50 → 31 and cells inside another tag's cluster 152 → 58; the meter's edges cross more (crossings 105 → 121). Only pieces with tags inside them change. **forces** has the new strengths under *clusters*. Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- The graph (#27): a turntable's name (tape, motor, belt, resonator) sits on a pill like a cell's, so an edge or edge label that passes it runs underneath, not through the text. Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- Wheels (#107): the playhead is white with a dark edge (it was the live edges' yellow, and got lost where they cross); a spectrum at rest shows its partials brighter, inside a rim; an envelope names its values on its curve (A 3 ms, D 180 ms, S 10 %, R 60 ms) instead of four bare numbers; a braid's sheet writes long numbers to three figures (2.3429 → 2.34; the code keeps what you wrote). Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- The graph (#27): a tape's heads face what they connect to. The heads keep their spacing round the wheel, but the wheel is drawn turned (the write head is no longer always on top), so each edge leaves its head straight out, towards what that head records or plays (Echo Tape, Sound on Tape, Test Tones on Tape, Resonanz, 5 to 12). Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- The graph untangles more (#27): crossing edges push apart harder and edges pull a little less tightly, so the examples cross about a quarter less (106 crossings over the library instead of 142; BWV 847 28 → 9, Cellscape 33 → 26, Pop 7 → 3), for about 6 % more room. Every piece's graph moves a little; the library's miniatures with it. The old strengths can be set in **forces** (crossing push 0.3, contraction 0.9). Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- Mixes follow one algebra ([decision 006](docs/decisions/006-mix-algebra.md), #10). The voice of a product is the factor that is a sound, wherever it's written, so `&lead * &theme` is `&theme * &lead`, and `(&theme * &vib) * &lead` groups as it reads. `+` and `*` are commutative and associative; a number is semitones in a sum and a gain in a product, in any position. The examples are unchanged (their events are identical). **Saved `.cells` pieces:** a cell played as a voice that has no sound parameter and no audio-rate `loop=` (e.g. `loop=5`, or no `loop=` at all) is now a pitch, and its mix says so with an error; so is a pattern multiplied by its own kit (`&drums * &kit`: mix `&drums`).
- Attributes in the wrong place are warnings now (#10): an attribute on a cell where it does nothing (`swing=` on a voice, `level=` off the sound card, `wow=` without a motor, `ratio=` on a voice no `fm=` names, `title=` on a cell that plays …), `swing=` on a `scan()` meter (it only swings a list or `meter()`), and a word where a number belongs on a cell that isn't a voice (`motor=strong`) said nothing before. Dough's other names (`cutoff=`, `reverb=` …) still work, with a warning naming ours. A misspelt attribute is answered with the nearest one (`stpe=`: did you mean `step=`?), and the list of attributes in that message is complete. The node editor offers `about=`. Saved `.cells` pieces play as before (no example changes: their events are identical); a piece may show new warnings.
- The library opens without fetching or compiling any example (#61): each piece's graph in miniature is worked out at build time and kept in the examples' index, so a piece's text is fetched only when it opens. Until every miniature is drawn: about 1.7 s instead of 3.9 s here, 6.3 s instead of 14.9 s at a 4× CPU slowdown; the first download is about 7 kB larger gzipped. The miniatures look the same. Saved `.cells` pieces and opened files still draw theirs in the browser and are not affected.
- The audio output is tuned for lower latency: `balanced` instead of `playback`, measured on an Android phone with the full latency test at 56 ms from made to heard instead of 280 ms (desktop ~45 ms instead of ~85 ms), with no late or left-out notes on the heaviest pieces. Hands (strike, bow) are heard sooner by the same amount. If a piece clicks on a device, `?latency=playback` brings the old setting back (kept until `?latency=off`). Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected; how a piece sounds is not, only when.
- Library cards (#12): a tap anywhere on a piece's wheel opens it (in the graph: its details); before, a tap on a tag round the rim filtered by that tag instead. Tags are chosen from the chips. The cards' titles use the card's width, the wheel's petals (cells, length) are no longer cut off on a phone, and **Files** sits beside the heading. A piece's details keep the keyboard focus while open and give it back on closing.
- The library, choosing and seeing (#12): the header shows the open piece's place in the library as a path, *kind › tag › piece* (`artist › Neubauten › Baustelle II`), each step a dropdown — a quick way to a neighbour. The cards' tag row (40-odd chips) is one dropdown per kind (genre, artist, topic), and choices in several hold together. In the library's graph the tags organise the layout, grown from the middle: tiny nodes gather by their tags, then grow until the wheel is full, pushing only their neighbours — the library's opening animation (replayed from the kept layout on later visits; still with reduced motion), at the full view each piece is its title, a pill, and zooming in grows its wheel (with its miniature, fetched only then) behind it, its cells and cycle in a row under the title; a phone opens zoomed in to where the titles read, and the shading is one wash per tag, as in a piece's graph (the haloes and shaded lines are gone; a lit tag ties its pieces with lines). The library is laid out afresh once (its kept layout is from the old forces). Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- `bwv847` is written shorter (#82): `tools/bwv847.py` now runs its longhand through Shorten (`tools/shorten.mjs`), so repeated phrases are cells of their own, used exactly or transposed (10,138 → 9,999 characters in the committed file). The notes are unchanged (checked note by note, and the events of every example hash the same); the graph has one more shared cell and a few more crossings (24 → 28). The order convention is written down in `docs/language/display.md`.

### Fixed
- A fast vibrato works in a mix again (#28): a braid turning at an audio rate with no sound parameter (`&vib loop=30 …`) is a pitch in a pitch sum or beside another sound, `(&theme + &vib) * &lead`, as it was as `pitch=&vib`; alone it is still a waveform voice. Since the mix algebra (#111) it was refused as "a sound". This also makes FM writable in the algebra: `(&tune + &op) * &lead`. No example changes.
- Reading through a table of cells says what happens (#29): `&solo over=&tunes` where `&tunes &a &b` lists melodies played only 0s, silently (a table is its numbers; the cells are skipped). It now warns, and says to splice the cells into a braid. Saved `.cells` pieces play as before.
- One geometry for every wheel (#106): a cell's wheel, a spectrum, a waveform and the braid editor share their hub, rim, disc, border and playhead, so they line up side by side; the hub is a little larger on a cell's wheel, the spectrum's ring a little narrower, and the braid editor sits on a disc like the rest with the same playhead and lit step. The braid's line editor (tap a braid's hub) takes the keyboard focus when it opens, keeps it inside, and gives it back when it closes; Escape there closes only it, no longer the cell's sheet as well. Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- Small wheels (#105): a wheel's text fits it at every size. On petals and other small wheels the hub shows its number, or nothing when it can't be read, instead of a label spilling over the rim; the second line, the shift and the beat numbers show from 80 (beats 64) px up; the rim's ticks, notch and playhead scale with the wheel. Library wheels under ~94 px show their tags as a band without names; the braid editor's step labels fit their segments. Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- Wheels (#104): a piece with a single tag shows its tag ring and name in the library again; the sun and the turntables can be turned from the keyboard when stopped (arrows: an hour, a sixteenth of a turn; Enter or Space opens a turntable), and the sheet's turning rim is announced as a slider; a resonator no longer shimmers with reduced motion. Nothing else looks different; saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- `off` is off (#76): the WAV export and `cellStudio.measure`/`render` now leave out switched-off cells (they played everything), and in playback a switched-off tape, tape head, resonator, or mix no longer sounds, records, rings or drives a resonator. Pieces with nothing switched off sound the same; a saved `.cells` piece that has an `off` cell on a tape, head or resonator now really goes quiet there.
- What you see matches what you hear (#67): the position, the highlighted notes and the lit cells and edges now follow the sound as it leaves the speaker, not as the audio engine makes it, so they no longer run ahead of it (by 280 ms on the phone measured). The scopes and spectra, which read the signal as it is made, still run ahead of the speaker by the output latency (about 55 ms with the new default). Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- The graph shows what sounds, for nodes and edges alike (#11): cells that sound are no longer drawn dimmed (a cell that moves a filter or a pitch, a part made only of chance choices, a mix used as an instrument such as Sound on Tape's `&dry`, the mix of tape heads that drives Resonanz's pipes); tapes, resonators, motors and scopes now fade too when off or unused; a selector lights only the part it picks now, and the way from it to the sound card; an edge takes a note's colour and pulses only while what it carries plays (a voice in two mixes no longer colours the edge to the one that isn't playing). The sound and saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.

### Changed
- Lighter playback, fourth round: the transport clock and every live view (scopes, spectrum wheels, tapes, resonators) share one frame loop, which spreads the views' redraws over frames instead of letting each run its own, and each clock's timing is worked out once per piece instead of on every lookup. On the test machine the main thread is 1–4 points less busy on most pieces at full speed. The look, the sound and saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- Three examples no longer clip: Zelluläre Aktivität is 9 dB quieter (it peaked 7.5 dB over full scale and distorted in its build-up), Pop song 1 dB quieter and Music Box 2 dB quieter, so each peaks under −1 dB. Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- Seven examples had a placeholder `level=0` and now have a measured level, so they sit with the rest of the library at about −24 dB average: Waveforms +0.5, Drunken Sailor −1.5, IDM −0.5, Acid-Cell +0.5, One more Cell −0.5, Ghost-Cells −1 and Cellular Brothers −3 dB (it was clipping; its peak is now −1 dB). Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- Lighter playback on phones, third round: the redraw after each clock tick is about a third cheaper on the busiest piece (wheels recompute only what changed — the lit step, the entries read, the label's bar — and a step that makes an edge glow no longer rebuilds the whole edge list). The look is unchanged.
- The examples are no longer all in the first download: the library is listed from an index made at build time (nothing is compiled to show it), and each piece's text is fetched when its card is drawn or it is opened (the first bundle is about 24 kB smaller gzipped). If a piece can't be fetched (offline, never opened before), it says so instead of opening. Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- Cell studio is now an installable app (PWA): it has an icon and a manifest, works offline after the first visit, and shows "New version ready" when an update is waiting (it never reloads by itself, so a playing piece is not cut off). The build is no longer one `index.html` but normal files with Tone loaded on first Play. Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.

### Fixed
- Noise voices (`s=white`, `pink`, `brown`) and a resonator's bow (`body=`) no longer draw on `Math.random()`: the noise itself and where each noise note starts in it (from the cell's `seed` and the note's place in the piece, live or rendered) are the same every time, so renders can be compared. Every noise note's grain differs from before, but the sound is the same kind. Tone's reverb and a tape's hiss still use `Math.random()`, and Chromium sums inputs in a changing order, so two renders agree to float rounding (about −180 dB), not bit for bit. Saved `.cells` pieces are not affected.
- A kit's members now use their own `env=`: a drum read through a kit (`&drums over=&kit …`) used to ignore its envelope and ring for the whole step. This changes how **Drunken Sailor**, **IDM: drill**, **Automaton** and **Metronome** sound (their drums are as short as written); the levels of Drunken Sailor and Automaton were re-set to keep their loudness. Saved pieces that read a kit whose members have `env=` will sound different too.
- **Metronome** no longer hisses: its clicks are short tonal ticks (higher and longer on the downbeat, lower and shorter for finer divisions) instead of noise bursts.
- Opening a piece now starts it from rest: the transport stops at 0 and nothing sounds, even if another piece was playing (it used to carry on from where it was) or the new piece has tapes (their hiss sounded as soon as the piece opened).
